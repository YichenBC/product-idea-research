# Competitor research: compare a task, not feature counts

Start with the user's task and decision: choose a tool, understand a named product,
position an idea, or identify an unmet workflow. A competitor-only request can end
with a comparison and recommendation; no personas or social research are required.
User reviews used to assess a competitor do not automatically require a separate
social run. Record the work under the objective actually performed.

## Select alternatives and comparison dimensions

Cover alternatives that plausibly solve the task:

- Direct: products targeting the same job.
- Indirect: broader tools or an adjacent service assembled into a workflow.
- Manual: existing spreadsheets, scripts, outsourcing or a human service.
- Do nothing: defer or tolerate the issue when its cost is low.

Do not invent a competitor to fill all four categories. If manual/do-nothing
options are your own hypotheses without sources, discuss them as next-step
questions; do not fabricate a cited comparison row.

Choose a few dimensions that change the decision: supported input/output,
quality/control, collaboration/review, integrations, language coverage, deployment,
data handling, accessibility, learning/setup effort and total task cost as relevant.
“Has AI” is rarely useful by itself. Different products may serve different users;
explain mismatch rather than declaring one universally best.

## Evidence ladder

Use official documentation, pricing, changelogs and product pages for what vendors
say they offer. Read relevant details, not only the homepage headline. Before
recommending a differentiator, search the closest incumbent's documentation for
that exact workflow and inspect adjacent sections: comments, revisions, approval
and export capabilities may exist outside the main feature description. Include
the strongest overlapping feature in the comparison and next-test baseline.
Preserve modality: optional configuration or “should” is not enforced permission
or “must”. A source qualification must survive paraphrasing. Use public
user experiences for reported frictions, with self-report/context caveats. If the
user authorized product exploration and the host has access, inspect a concrete
workflow and describe exactly what you observed. Never imply hands-on testing from
reading a vendor page. Do not start trials, purchase, upload private materials or
contact sales just to fill cells without the necessary authorization.

Current pricing and capabilities must be retrieved in this run or their retrieval
age disclosed. Record date, plan, currency, billing period, unit, included allowance
and relevant overage/limits together. An annual-billing monthly equivalent is not
month-to-month pricing. “Contact sales” is unknown cost. Distinguish transcription
minutes, translation minutes, dubbing credits, seats and exports. No estimated
cost arithmetic without stated assumptions and compatible units.

Follow the source contract from [social research](social.md#save-evidence-not-reconstructed-testimony):
original URL, actual access depth, excerpt/locator, date and limitations. Keep
vendor assertions as assertions. Missing evidence becomes “not established in the
reviewed sources”; it does not mean the feature is absent. A source about another
plan, platform or date cannot establish this product's current limit.

## Deliver a useful comparison

Use optional report `comparisons`, one row per alternative + dimension. Each row
has a concise assessment, source-only support and limitations. Link exact excerpts.
Unknowns may be expressed when the cited material's scope explains the gap;
otherwise omit the unsupported row and state a research limitation. Keep detailed
plan conditions and citation material in the evidence companion.

Then synthesize the consequences rather than repeating the table:

- Which task/constraint each alternative serves best in the reviewed material.
- Where an apparent gap is already solved by an existing tool or cheap workaround.
- Remaining uncertainty, switching costs, and what requires a real workflow test.
- For an opportunity request, a narrow differentiator and a falsifiable next test.

A claim that “no competitor does X” requires much broader evidence than a few
product pages. Prefer “X was not established for these alternatives in this pass.”
Retain contrary results even when they remove the proposed opportunity.

Save a competitor run with actual queries, method and selected sources. Complete
means the declared comparison scope is covered; unknown mandatory dimensions,
blocked requested products or a missed user's count leave it partial. Existing
runs remain immutable so a later price refresh produces a new comparable snapshot.
