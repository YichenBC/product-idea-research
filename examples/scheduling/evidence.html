Recipe: r-schedule

Question: When would a small team need more than a shared rota spreadsheet?

Compare a frontline employee satisfied with a stable rota and an approver handling frequent changes. Explore rejection as well as adoption.

Dimensions

  • schedule: stable, changing

Constraints

  • No browsing or real customer claims

Full simulated transcripts

Interview: i-employee

Provenance

Persona: Employee with a stable rota (p-employee)

Turn 1

Question: In this scenario, how would you check your next shift?

Answer: I would open the shared sheet. With a stable rota, that seems sufficient.

Assumptions

None supplied

Turn 2

Question: What change could make that insufficient?

Answer: If someone edited a shift after I checked it, I would need a clear notification rather than another planning tool.

Assumptions

  • A shift can change after the weekly check

Turn 3

Question: Would you want a separate scheduling app solely for notifications?

Answer: Not necessarily. If a simple message linked to the updated sheet worked, I would prefer to keep that workflow.

Assumptions

None supplied

Interview: i-approver

Provenance

Persona: Supervisor handling schedule changes (p-approver)

Turn 1

Question: How would you coordinate a late swap under these assumptions?

Answer: I would check both people agree and then update the approved rota.

Assumptions

None supplied

Turn 2

Question: What could make a dedicated tool useful?

Answer: An explicit pending-versus-approved state could help if messages and the rota diverged. The simulation does not establish how often that happens.

Assumptions

  • A proposed swap may be mistaken for an approved swap

Turn 3

Question: What would make you reject it?

Answer: I would reject a tool that requires updating both its schedule and the sheet. I would also need owner approval before agreeing to a recurring cost.

Assumptions

None supplied
